# EVM Group homepage v2 (evm2/)

- **Mode:** inspiration (owner: "I love its design language, but inspiration, not a copy"). Reference: esrbespoke.au. Owner approved the plan and chose hero 1 (night drive, WebGL).
- **Inputs:** the reference homepage; EVM content from the earlier build (`evm/`), because evmgroup.co.in could not be read (expired TLS certificate, a 503, and an Angular shell that needs JavaScript). Wayback Machine was blocked by the session's network policy; Pixabay's site returned 403 and the key the owner pasted returned 401.
- **Spine:** one road through Kerala. A red rail line with a headlamp and a kilometre readout runs down the page: 000 at Maradu, up to Munnar (about 135), back to 000 at the footer ("the road starts and ends at home"). Sections are stops: night drive, lagoon (1952), mileposts (marques), the years rolling like an odometer on a drawn road (milestones), the climb (five businesses, a red sun rises, the tea photo opens from the sun), people on paper, odometer numbers, arrival (giant EVM mark under a sweeping headlamp).
- **Palette and type:** night-lagoon green-black, paper, EVM red only as road, headlamp, sun and mark, tea green in the climb. Bricolage Grotesque (one weight, 600) and Hanken Grotesk.
- **Hero:** procedural three.js road (custom shader, bend, posts with reflectors, beams, dust, horizon lights). Pointer steers, scroll adds speed. Needed no footage, so no spend.
- **Checks:** Impeccable on built HTML (real: leading, tracking, one contrast; ignored with reason: 19 hairline-row "cramped padding", 3 glows that depict a headlamp, sun and rail lamp, large display leading); detail audit clean apart from digit stacks; media quality: no upscaled files, full-bleed photos 0.70 scale; motion probe 191 vs ESR 272 moving elements (70%), no motionless section.
- **Weaker:** page is about 16 viewports (1.9x ESR) because of pinned scenes; photography is only two CC0 stock photos and four small portraits; milestone and odometer sections are type moments; software WebGL was used for all screenshots so real-GPU feel is unmeasured.
- **Lessons:** (1) a hero that is code (procedural WebGL) avoids the stock-footage quality trap and costs nothing. (2) The spine worked best as a persistent instrument (the rail) plus a changing scene, not as decoration. (3) gsap and CSS transforms conflict: never pre-set `transform` in CSS on an element gsap animates (the headline tween silently did nothing). (4) The probe counts elements, so split-text motion counts; per-letter motion is both better design and a fairer score.
