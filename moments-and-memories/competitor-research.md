# Competitor research: what similar companies do

Reviewed 2026-10-13: the homepage of each site, desktop and mobile, with screenshots and a scripted check of navigation, calls to action, contact options, prices, reviews, safety/insurance wording, forms and structured data. Raw data: `assets/competitors/analysis.json`. Screenshots: `assets/competitors/`.

**How far to trust this:** it is a small sample (4 direct competitors and 2 quote marketplaces) taken from search results, homepages only. It shows patterns, not what the whole market does. It tells us what competitors show customers, not how well it converts. Search results were US-biased and found no hirer based in Borehamwood itself, so "local competitor" here means a nearby company in Hertfordshire or North London.

## Patterns worth borrowing
1. **Quote and message options above the fold.** The strongest site puts "Get an instant quote", "WhatsApp us" and its phone number in a bar at the top of every page, and repeats them on mobile. Lisa's site has none of these as buttons (finding M-05).
2. **A speed promise.** "Your quote will be sent within 60 seconds" gives a reason to enquire now. Lisa should only promise what she can keep (for example "same day").
3. **Navigate by what the customer is planning.** Party in Your Garden uses "Event type" and "Marquee sizes". Customers can start from their situation, not the supplier's catalogue. Moments and Memories' four services map onto this.
4. **Prices up front.** Two of the four competitors show prices on the homepage. Where prices are missing the customer has to enquire to compare. "From £" prices would make Lisa easier to shortlist (M-06).
5. **Proof near the first action.** A review count with a Google badge, galleries and case studies sit beside the main button.
6. **Seasonal pages.** Party in Your Garden has "Special offers 2026/2027" and "Christmas/NYE events". With Christmas and New Year parties being booked now, an equivalent page is a fast, timely win.
7. **Local wording and markup.** Watford Marquees puts "Watford, Hertfordshire & North West London" into its title and H1 and uses LocalBusiness markup, even though its page is thin (99 words). It is the simplest local-search signal and Lisa's site has none of it (M-01, M-04).

## What not to copy
- **Visual clutter.** Active Softplay is busy (cartoon art, clouds, banners, payment logos) and Party in Your Garden relies on a cookie-blocked video and a pop-up. A calm, premium layout suits Lisa's cocktail and wedding work better.
- **Thin pages.** Watford Marquees' homepage has very little copy; it doesn't help customers decide.
- **Marketplace dependence.** On Add to Event and Eventsense the customer compares several suppliers at once, so reply speed and clear information decide who wins. Lisa's own site must make the same decision easy.

## Where Moments and Memories could stand out
Among the sites reviewed, each covers one slice: marquees, bouncy castles, or entertainment hire. None of them presented children's parties, marquees, cocktail bars and weddings as one all-in-one service. That matches Lisa's own line ("everything under one roof"), but it only becomes a reason to choose her if the site proves it with real events, reviews and clear packages. This is an opening, not a confirmed advantage, because the sample is small.
