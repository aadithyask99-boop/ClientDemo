| Funtime Bounce | Very light page, GA4, prices per product |
| Add to Event, Eventsense | Marketplaces with GA4, Trustpilot or review widgets, request-a-quote flows |

**What this tells us:** the strongest competitor runs the **same stack as Lisa** (WordPress and Elementor). Their advantage is **not technology**. It is what they put on the page: quote and WhatsApp everywhere, prices, proof, local wording, plus tracking. That makes the gap cheap to close.

## 3. What competitors lack (A, small sample; H where marked)
- **No all-in-one story.** Each covers one slice (marquees, bouncy castles, or entertainment hire). None of the sites reviewed presented kids' parties, marquees, cocktail bars and weddings together. Possible positioning for Lisa, but only if she proves it (H).
- **Cluttered or thin pages.** Active Softplay is very busy. Watford Marquees' homepage is about 99 words with no prices.
- **Friction on mobile.** On Party in Your Garden, a cookie pop-up covers the first screen and its main video won't show until cookies are accepted.
- **Safety and insurance wording** not found on two of four homepages (H: may be elsewhere on the site).
- **Marketplaces compete on speed:** the customer sends one request and gets several quotes. Slow or vague replies lose the job (R: [Add to Event](https://www.addtoevent.co.uk/)).

## 4. How Lisa can win without a big budget
1. **Be easy to ask:** quote, WhatsApp and call on every page. Reply fast, and say how fast only if she can keep it.
2. **Be easy to judge:** "from" prices, real photos, reviews, safety and insurance.
3. **Be easy to find nearby:** Google Business Profile, local title and headline, structured data.
4. **Own the "one planner" angle:** a single contact for the party, the marquee and the cocktail bar.
5. **Measure it:** call, WhatsApp and form tracking, so we can show what the site produces.

## 5. How we present the solution (ladder)
1. **Call:** diagnose with her own numbers (where enquiries come from, reply speed, prices, area).
2. **Free 15-minute website check:** our existing entry offer. The audit is the evidence.
3. **Proposal:** Starter from £999 plus VAT (50/50), with the scope split above.
4. **After launch:** Care (£599 a month plus VAT) for visibility, reviews and posts. Growth when she has budget for ads.

## 6. Risks and things to confirm
- Budget and timing (H): she asked about "aggressive growth" without a retainer. Don't oversell Growth.

- Whether she wants a rebuild (Starter) or fixes to the existing site (Redesign and Optimisation).

### Proposed structure for the call brief (one page, plus a script)
Adapted from the account-brief template and the cold-call guidance in marketingskills:
1. **Who and why now:** name, business, source of the lead, what she asked for (her words), date.
2. **Fit and lead score:** active business, category, area, website status, contact channel verified, score (hot, warm, cold).
3. **Business snapshot:** services, area, size, presence (site, Google Business Profile, social), recent activity from the LinkedIn screenshots.
4. **Three audit headlines** she will recognise, each with evidence.
5. **Opportunity and angle:** one sentence connecting her situation to our offer.
6. **Discovery questions** (the "to validate" list) and **what not to say** (stale, sensitive or creepy points).
7. **Offer and price guardrails:** Starter, Care, Growth, with what is and is not included.
8. **Objections and answers:** budget, "I have a site", "I'll do it myself", "send me an email".
9. **Call plan:** four beats opener (who, why, permission, question), goal (book the next step, not sell), close.
10. **Follow-up:** email draft and a place to log the outcome.
11. **Compliance line:** source, date, lawful basis, TPS and consent check.
